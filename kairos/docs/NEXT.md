# Next: ideas to explore

Not built, and not promised. Each one starts from something a partner would plausibly want after the first few days. The shape of the app is one idea, one tap and a clear ending, so anything here should keep that.

## Closing the loop on an idea

- **How did she take it?** After “I did it”, an optional one-tap answer (loved it, fine, not really). Today the planner only knows what was done, not what landed; this would let it learn what works for *her*.
- **What did she say?** For the ideas that start a conversation (“ask about a dream she hasn’t mentioned”), a one-line note saved as something she mentioned, which the planner already knows how to bring back later.
- **Still today?** An opt-in evening reminder at about six, only if today’s idea is not done and was not skipped.

## Seeing the pattern

- **A look back.** A quiet Sunday-evening summary: ideas done this week, what she mentioned, what is coming. No streaks and no scores; proof of effort, not a game.
- **Her seasons, explained once.** A two-screen guide to what winter, spring, summer and autumn mean for her, reachable from the season name, for the partner who wants to understand rather than just act.

## Making an idea easy to do

- **One-tap hand-offs.** A drafted message, a table search, a calendar block or a shopping list from the idea itself (the plan already carries an action for some ideas).
- **Dates that matter.** Her birthday, their anniversary, her mother’s birthday: bigger ideas with real lead time, set once.

## More ways in

- **A glance.** A Home Screen widget and a watch tap showing today’s idea, and a Siri shortcut for “I did it”.
- **Her side.** Pairing with her phone (the paired edition), so she sees what he sees, decides what to share and can send him a signal.
- **Second-day setup.** A short “tell us about her” card on the second day (her flowers, food, music) once he has seen what an idea looks like, instead of asking before he has.
